Repiping older homes

Spokane has a lot of beautiful old houses. Plenty of them on the South Hill, in Browne's Addition, the Perry District and around Gonzaga still have the galvanized steel water pipe they were built with. It doesn't last forever, and by now most of it is done.

Signs you have galvanized pipe that's failing

  • Weak water pressure, worst upstairs or when two things run at once.
  • Brown or rusty water, especially first thing in the morning.
  • Small leaks starting to show up at the joints.
  • The house was built before about 1960 and nobody has redone the plumbing.

Not sure what you have? Look at the pipe where it comes into your water heater. Galvanized is dull gray and threaded at the joints, and a magnet will stick to it. Copper is orange-brown. PEX is plastic, usually red, blue or white.

What we do

We replace the old supply lines with PEX, or with copper if you'd prefer it or the pipe will be exposed. We plan the route ahead of time so we only open the walls we have to, and we work carefully around original trim and plaster. We've done this in enough 100-year-old houses to know where the surprises usually are.

You don't have to move out. Your water is off during the day and back on every night.

Sometimes a full repipe isn't needed yet. If only the main line or one bathroom is the problem, we'll tell you, and you can do the rest later.

Questions we hear a lot

How long does it take?

Most 1 to 2 bath houses take 2 to 3 days. We'll give you a schedule before we start.

Who fixes the holes in the walls?

We keep them small and we put things back neatly, but we don't finish drywall or plaster. We work with a local plaster and drywall guy who knows old houses, and we can schedule him right behind us.

Is PEX as good as copper?

For most houses, yes. It handles freezing better, has fewer joints, and costs less to put in. Copper is still a good choice where the pipe will be exposed or if you just prefer it. We'll put in either one.

We're buying an older house. Should we have the plumbing checked?

Yes. Ask your inspector to note the pipe type, and get a sewer scope too. Our sewer lines page covers that.
